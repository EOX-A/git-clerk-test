By measuring atmospheric carbon dioxide, retreating ice sheets, rising sea levels, and catastrophic weather events from hundreds of kilometers above, spaceborne sensors transform subtle planetary shifts into concrete scientific evidence.

## The Thin Blue Veil: Greenhouse Gases in the Atmosphere

<!--{ as="eox-layout" layout="split" }-->

Human activities have substantially altered the chemical equilibrium of Earth's atmosphere. Since the pre-industrial era, atmospheric carbon dioxide concentrations have surged past 420 parts per million—levels unprecedented in at least 800,000 years.

Instruments like the Copernicus Sentinel-5P TROPOMI sensor and NASA's Orbiting Carbon Observatory (OCO-2 and OCO-3) map concentrations of methane ($CH_4$), carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), and nitrogen dioxide ($NO_2$) with exceptional spatial resolution. These measurements reveal point sources of emissions, from fossil fuel power generation facilities to intensive agricultural basins and leaking fossil infrastructure.

## Polar Realities: The Fragile Cryosphere

<!--{ as="eox-map" mode="tour" projection="EPSG:4326" zoom=2 center=[0,20] }-->

### <!--{ zoom=3.5 center=[-41.5,72.0] animationOptions='{"duration":1000}' }--> The Melting Greenland Ice Sheet
The Greenland Ice Sheet holds enough frozen water to raise global sea levels by over seven meters. Radar altimetry missions such as CryoSat-2 and Sentinel-3 track the accelerating surface melt and glacier thinning, recording average mass losses of over 270 billion tons of ice per year.

### <!--{ zoom=3 center=[0.0,-82.0] animationOptions='{"duration":1000}' }--> Antarctic Shelf Destabilization
Along the Antarctic Peninsula and West Antarctica, warm ocean currents undermine floating ice shelves from below. As ice shelves collapse, inland glaciers accelerate toward the ocean, contributing directly to global sea level rise.

### <!--{ zoom=3.5 center=[80.0,0.0] animationOptions='{"duration":1000}' }--> Diminishing Arctic Sea Ice Extent
Summer Arctic sea ice extent has shrunk by roughly 12% per decade since satellite monitoring began in 1979. The replacement of reflective bright ice with dark ocean water creates a positive feedback loop known as Arctic amplification, accelerating warming across the northern hemisphere.

## Transforming Landscapes: Drought, Fire, and Vegetation Stress

Rising global temperatures and shifts in precipitation patterns drive intense mega-droughts and severe wildfire seasons across multiple continents.

Using multi-spectral optical instruments like Sentinel-2 and Landsat 8/9, scientists track vegetation health using indices like the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) and Normalized Burn Ratio (NBR). These indices detect severe moisture stress before die-offs occur and quantify ecosystem recovery following wildfires.

## Oceans in Motion: Thermal Expansion and Coastal Vulnerability

Oceans absorb over 90% of the excess heat trapped in the climate system by greenhouse gas emissions. Satellites equipped with microwave radiometers measure Sea Surface Temperature (SST) anomalies that fuel severe tropical cyclones, bleach coral reef ecosystems, and disrupt marine trophic webs.

Simultaneously, radar altimeters demonstrate that global mean sea level is rising at more than 3.7 millimeters per year—driven jointly by thermal expansion of warming water and mass loss from land-based glaciers and ice sheets.
