## A Planet in Transition: The View from Space
Observations collected by European and international constellation satellites document an undeniable acceleration in climate impacts across every continent and ocean basin. According to comprehensive findings by the [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/) and sustained observations from the [Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S)](https://climate.copernicus.eu/), human-induced global surface temperatures have reached historic anomalies relative to pre-industrial benchmarks.

Spaceborne sensors measure key climate variables with daily frequency, capturing the twin signals of environmental stress and systemic resilience. From diminishing polar sea-ice extents to intensified heatwaves, prolonged mega-droughts, and marine heat stress, Earth observation provides the quantitative baseline required to evaluate both risks and solutions. Satellite telemetry informs global renewable deployments, carbon sequestration verification, wetland restoration, and urban heat adaptation, establishing a direct link between planetary data and local mitigation action.

## Scaling Solutions: From Monitoring to Transformation
Addressing anthropogenic climate disruption requires scaling rapid decarbonization alongside ecological restoration and community resilience. As underscored in the [IPCC Working Group III Synthesis](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/), existing technologies—including solar photovoltaics, offshore and onshore wind, electrified transport, and forest-wetland conservation—offer viable, cost-effective pathways to curb emissions across every sector.

Sustained Earth observation constellations will remain pivotal in measuring progress: independently auditing national greenhouse gas inventories, tracking reforestation carbon stocks, and validating clean energy infrastructure rollouts worldwide.
