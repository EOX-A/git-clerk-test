# Monitoring the Global Oceans from Space

### Harnessing Satellite Observations to Track Marine Dynamics, Sea Surface Temperatures, and Coastal Ecosystems

The Earth's oceans cover more than 70% of the planet's surface, acting as the primary climate regulator by absorbing heat and carbon dioxide. Understanding ocean circulation, surface anomalies, and coastal ecosystems requires continuous, high-resolution Earth Observation (EO) data streams collected by spaceborne sensors.

#### Tracking the Mediterranean Basin
The Mediterranean Sea serves as a miniature ocean laboratory. With intense maritime traffic, sensitive coastal habitats, and accelerating warming trends, synthetic aperture radar (SAR) and optical sensors track surface currents, biogenic slicks, and coastal water clarity.

#### Atlantic Equatorial Dynamics
Across the open Atlantic, satellite altimetry and radiometry measure sea surface temperature anomalies and wave heights. These measurements feed predictive models forecasting extreme weather and monitoring changes in global thermohaline circulation.

#### Coral Ecosystems & Indo-Pacific Currents
In the Indo-Pacific, multi-spectral satellite imagery enables continuous coral reef habitat mapping and detects thermal stress events before large-scale bleaching occurs.

## The Role of Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)

Optical sensors are frequently hindered by persistent marine cloud cover. Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), such as Sentinel-1, penetrates clouds day and night to map sea ice extent, wind fields, vessel activity, and surface roughness anomalies across the open ocean and remote polar seas.
