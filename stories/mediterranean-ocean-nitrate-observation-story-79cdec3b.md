## Satellite Perspectives on Oceanic Productivity and Biogeochemistry
Understanding climate change dynamics in the marine environment—spanning ocean surface temperature anomalies, wind-driven circulation, and nutrient transport—is essential for the sustainable management of global marine resources. These physical shifts directly drive changes in base trophic organisms: phytoplankton and zooplankton, invertebrate larvae, and pelagic fish populations across regional and global scales.

## Conclusions & Data Attribution
Long-term monitoring of oceanic nutrients and primary productivity is fundamental to evaluating the health and carbon absorption capacity of marine ecosystems under ongoing climatic disruption. Harmonizing spaceborne multispectral ocean color radiometric observations with numerical biogeochemical models delivers unprecedented insight into ecological shifts across global ocean regimes.
