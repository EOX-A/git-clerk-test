## Eyes on the Global Ocean
The Earth's oceans absorb over 90% of the excess heat accumulated in the climate system and approximately a quarter of anthropogenic carbon dioxide emissions. Systematic spaceborne observation platforms, led by the [Copernicus Marine Service](https://marine.copernicus.eu/) and international altimetry constellations, provide essential climate variables (ECVs) across planetary basins.

From thermal microwave radiometers on Sentinel-3 to radar altimetry missions like Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich, satellite oceanography captures sea surface height (SSH), geostrophic currents, sea surface temperature (SST), and marine chlorophyll-a concentrations with daily global coverage. These observations allow marine scientists and operational meteorological services to detect extreme marine heatwaves, trace nutrient upwelling cycles along continental shelves, and monitor dynamic boundary currents such as the Gulf Stream, the Agulhas Current, and the Kuroshio Extension.

#### The Agulhas Retroflection & Ring Shedding
South of the Cape of Good Hope, the Agulhas Current retroflects westward, shedding giant anticyclonic eddies that inject warm, saline Indian Ocean water into the South Atlantic corridor ([ESA Earth Online](https://earth.esa.int/)).

Persistent coastal wind-driven upwelling draws cold, nutrient-rich deep waters to the sunlit photic zone off South America, driving intense primary productivity tracked by [Copernicus Marine Service](https://marine.copernicus.eu/).

#### The Kuroshio Current and Extension
Transporting equatorial heat north-eastward past Japan into the Pacific basin, the Kuroshio jet features intense kinetic vorticity and prominent frontal meanders monitored by global altimetry networks ([Copernicus Marine Service](https://marine.copernicus.eu/)).

#### Subpolar Gyre & Deep Water Formation
In the subpolar North Atlantic and Labrador Sea, intense ocean-atmosphere thermal exchange drives cold, dense water sinking, underpinning the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation ([Copernicus Marine Service](https://marine.copernicus.eu/)).

## Sustained Ocean Monitoring & Climate Outlook
Continuous operational satellite observations remain the cornerstone for anticipating global ocean changes, from accelerating sea level rise to intensifying marine heatwaves. Synergistic assimilation of spaceborne radar altimetry, optical ocean colour, and thermal radiometric signatures into high-resolution biogeochemical models enables early detection of ecosystem stress and shifts in pelagic biodiversity.

Ongoing initiatives across European and international space agencies, including future high-resolution wide-swath interferometry and next-generation Copernicus Sentinel missions, will further refine observations of submesoscale eddies and coastal bathymetric interactions.
