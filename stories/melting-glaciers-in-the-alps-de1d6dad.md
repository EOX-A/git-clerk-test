## The Disappearing Cryosphere of the European Alps
The European Alps are warming at more than twice the global average rate, triggering unprecedented ice volume loss and rapid glacier retreat. Systematic surveys conducted by alpine monitoring services and glaciological teams demonstrate that nearly all surveyed alpine glaciers have experienced sharp mass deficits in recent years, with long-standing glacier tongues collapsing into isolated ice fragments and newly formed proglacial lakes.

Spaceborne remote sensing via the [Copernicus Sentinel-2](https://sentinel.esa.int/web/sentinel/missions/sentinel-2) and [Copernicus Sentinel-1](https://sentinel.esa.int/web/sentinel/missions/sentinel-1) satellites plays an essential role in tracking glacier surface extent, snowline elevation, and terminal retreat. High-resolution optical multispectral bands enable researchers to map bare ice exposure, albedo changes from atmospheric dust deposition, and moraine instability across steep topography.

## Implications and Satellite Monitoring Outlook
The loss of alpine glacier volume has profound consequences for seasonal runoff, freshwater storage, hydropower production, and alpine slope stability. As ice tongues melt back, exposed debris and bedrock face increased hazard risks from rockfalls and glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs).

Continuous Earth observation constellations such as [Copernicus Sentinel-2](https://sentinel.esa.int/web/sentinel/missions/sentinel-2) and [Copernicus Sentinel-1](https://sentinel.esa.int/web/sentinel/missions/sentinel-1), combined with national digital twins like [GTIF Austria](https://gtif-austria.info), provide the spatio-temporal resolution necessary to track alpine change dynamics annually and support regional climate adaptation strategies.
