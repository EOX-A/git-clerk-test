## Monitoring Marine Ecosystems from Space
Understanding climate change effects in the marine environment—including shifting water temperatures, altered circulation pathways, and nutrient fluxes—is critical for sustainable marine governance and food security.

Microscopic primary producers such as phytoplankton form the foundation of marine food webs and govern oceanic carbon drawdown. As documented in the official dashboard narrative [Understanding climate change effects on water temperature and ocean currents is vital to managing these resources](https://ESA-eodashboards.github.io/eodashboard-narratives/ocean-colours.md), satellite sensors observing ocean color and biogeochemical dynamics track the timing, magnitude, and spatial extent of phytoplankton blooms. In highly productive systems like the Northwest Pacific Oyashio front, climate-driven temperature variations dictate whether spring blooms arrive earlier or later, directly altering predator recruitment and trophic energy transfer.

## Outlook & Data Integration
Long-term monitoring through satellite ocean color sensors and coupled biogeochemical reanalyses provides critical baselines for tracking climate change in marine ecosystems. Combining surface chlorophyll, phytoplankton functional types, and dissolved macronutrient fields empowers environmental researchers and fisheries managers to identify shifts in primary production cycles before ecosystem disruptions propagate up the food chain.
