## Marine Biogeochemistry and Global Ocean Monitoring
Understanding climate change impacts on ocean temperatures, circulation regimes, and biogeochemical cycling is fundamental to the sustainable stewardship of planetary marine resources. Shifts in physical oceanography directly modulate microscopic phytoplankton communities—the vital base of the marine food web—and govern the biological carbon pump responsible for atmospheric carbon dioxide uptake.

### Official Dashboard Narrative & Trilateral Context
This synthetic exploration builds upon the official trilateral Earth observation story [Understanding climate change effects on water temperature and ocean currents is vital to managing these resources](https://ESA-eodashboards.github.io/eodashboard-narratives/ocean-colours.md), published jointly by the European Space Agency ([ESA](https://www.esa.int)), [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/), and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency ([JAXA](https://global.jaxa.jp/)) on the [EO Dashboard](https://eodashboard.org/). The narrative highlights how satellite sensors observing ocean color monitor net primary productivity (NPP) in hyper-productive zones such as the Northwest Pacific Oyashio front, demonstrating critical correlations between water temperature shifts, bloom timing, and higher trophic level ecosystem vitality.

### Catalog Dataset Integration
To complement the ocean color observations featured in the official narrative, this story integrates the curated Earth observation catalog dataset **Ocean Nutrients (Nitrate)** (`copernicus-marine-nutrients`). Derived from Copernicus Marine Service biogeochemical forecast models, this layer quantifies nitrate ($NO_3$) concentrations, providing the biochemical perspective that drives the primary productivity variations identified by satellite radiometers.

#### Portuguese Coastal Upwelling System
Along the western Iberian shelf, persistent northerly winds drive coastal Ekman transport, triggering seasonal upwelling that brings cold, nitrate-rich North Atlantic deep water into the photic zone to nourish productive sardine and pelagic fisheries.

## Synthesis, Data Sources & Outlook
Continuous satellite remote sensing of ocean color combined with biogeochemical assimilations provides indispensable insight into the health and resilience of global marine ecosystems. By monitoring chlorophyll-a, net primary productivity, and nutrient regimes across macro- and regional scales, spaceborne observations equip scientists and policymakers to evaluate the cascading impacts of climate change on ocean biodiversity and fisheries sustainability.
