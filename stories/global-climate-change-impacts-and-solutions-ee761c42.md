## A Planet Under Pressure: The Satellite Perspective
Earth's climate system is undergoing rapid and profound transformation driven by anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions. According to the [Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S)](https://climate.copernicus.eu/), recent years have consistently broken global surface air temperature records, with average global temperatures pushing past 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels across multi-month stretches.

From the vantage point of Earth observation satellites—including the European Union's Copernicus Sentinel fleet and NASA's Earth Observing System—scientists capture high-precision, continuous measurements of essential climate variables (ECVs). These spaceborne instruments measure surface temperature anomalies, polar ice sheet elevation loss, rising sea levels via radar altimetry, and regional vegetative stress.

| Climate Indicator | Spaceborne Measurement System | Observed Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Surface Temperature | MODIS / Sentinel-3 SLSTR | Multi-year record breaking heatwaves |
| Ice Sheet Mass Loss | CryoSat-2 / ICESat-2 Altimetry | Accelerated discharge in Greenland & Antarctica |
| Sea Level Rise | Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich / Jason-3 | Global mean sea level rise exceeding 4.5 mm/year |
| Ecosystem Stress | Sentinel-2 MSI / Landsat 8-9 | Prolonged drought and altered vegetation phenology |

Satellite data provides objective, cross-border evidence that equips researchers and policymakers with the insights required to evaluate climate risks, implement adaptation strategies, and track decarbonization commitments under international frameworks like the [UNFCCC](https://unfccc.int/).

## Outlook: The Vital Role of Continuous Earth Observation
Sustained long-term satellite observations remain our most essential capability for understanding the rate and severity of global climate disruption. By monitoring the complex interplays between atmospheric chemistry, oceanic heat storage, terrestrial cryosphere collapse, and biosphere productivity, Earth observation systems transform abstract global trends into actionable, geographically specific data.

International Earth observation initiatives, such as the [Copernicus Earth Observation Programme](https://www.copernicus.eu/) and the [ESA Climate Change Initiative (CCI)](https://climate.esa.int/), generate freely accessible, calibrated climate records. These open archives serve as empirical foundations for IPCC assessments, national climate resilience roadmaps, and localized hazard mitigation.
