## Eyes on the Global Ocean: Remote Sensing and Marine Dynamics
The world's oceans cover more than 70% of the Earth's surface and absorb over 90% of excess heat generated by planetary warming. Monitoring oceanographic circulation, biogeochemical cycles, and atmospheric interaction requires systematic Earth Observation systems operating across microwave, infrared, and optical spectral bands.

Modern oceanography relies on coordinated constellations such as the [Copernicus Marine Service](https://marine.copernicus.eu/) and ESA's Sentinel missions. Altimetry instruments onboard Sentinel-3 and Sentinel-6 measure Sea Surface Height (SSH) with sub-centimeter accuracy, mapping geostrophic currents, planetary-scale Rossby waves, and dynamic mesoscale eddies. Concurrently, thermal infrared radiometers capture Sea Surface Temperature (SST) gradients, revealing upwelling mechanisms that sustain global fisheries and moderate regional microclimates.

From the high-energy currents of the Gulf Stream to the hyper-productive coastal upwelling off Benguela, satellite sensors provide uninterrupted time series that bridge regional marine ecology with planetary climate models.

The Gulf Stream carries warm tropical water northeastward into the North Atlantic, generating energetic warm- and cold-core rings tracked by satellite altimetry and [Copernicus Marine Service](https://marine.copernicus.eu/) sea surface temperature radiometers.

#### Southern Ocean: Drake Passage & Circumpolar Current
Unimpeded by continental barriers, the Antarctic Circumpolar Current connects the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian basins, acting as the primary regulator of global thermohaline redistribution and ocean carbon uptake.

## Sustaining the Global Marine Observing System
Continuous satellite observation of the marine environment is essential for assessing ocean acidification, accelerating deoxygenation, and diagnosing intensifying marine heatwaves. By assimilating spaceborne altimetry, radiometry, and synthetic aperture radar (SAR) data into numerical circulation models, oceanographers forecast coastal surges, guide sustainable fisheries, and assess the pace of sea level rise.

The upcoming generation of swath altimeters and hyperspectral ocean color missions promises to resolve sub-mesoscale ocean processes down to kilometer-scale features, opening new frontiers in understanding the biological carbon pump.
