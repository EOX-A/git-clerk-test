## Rising Temperatures in the Concrete Jungle
As summer heatwaves intensify worldwide, cities experience significantly higher thermal stress than surrounding rural areas—a phenomenon known as the **Urban Heat Island (UHI)** effect. Dense concentrations of asphalt, stone, steel, and concrete absorb solar radiation during daylight hours and re-radiate thermal energy throughout the night. Reduced vegetation canopy and sparse permeable soils diminish natural evaporative cooling, turning metropolitan centers into persistent thermal reservoirs.

Spaceborne thermal infrared sensors, including Landsat Thermal Infrared Sensors (TIRS) and the Copernicus Sentinel constellation, provide continuous monitoring of Land Surface Temperature (LST). Earth observation data allow urban planners, climatologists, and municipal authorities to map fine-scale microclimates, pinpoint vulnerable neighborhoods lacking tree cover, and quantify the cooling impact of green corridors, public parks, and urban water bodies.

## Mitigation and Spaceborne Climate Action
Addressing the urban heat challenge demands proactive adaptation strategies: expanding high-reflectance "cool roofs", planting shade-providing urban tree canopies, and restoring permeable unpaved grounds that enable evapotranspirative cooling. Remote sensing missions from the European Space Agency and Copernicus continue to deliver high-resolution thermal baselines essential for verifying urban greening initiatives and safeguarding vulnerable communities.
