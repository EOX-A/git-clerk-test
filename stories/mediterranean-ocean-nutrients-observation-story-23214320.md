## Satellite Perspectives on Marine Ecosystem Health
Understanding climate change effects across marine environments—including changing sea surface temperatures, altered ocean current regimes, and nutrient upwelling patterns—is vital for sustainable stewardship of marine ecosystems and food security.

### Official Dashboard Narrative & Thematic Context
This story builds upon insights from the official dashboard narrative:
- [Understanding climate change effects on water temperature and ocean currents is vital to managing these resources](https://ESA-eodashboards.github.io/eodashboard-narratives/ocean-colours.md) (from the [EO Dashboard](https://eodashboard.org/) trilateral collaboration between ESA, NASA, and JAXA).

As highlighted in the trilateral narrative, microscopic photosynthetic organisms—phytoplankton—form the foundational base of aquatic trophic webs. Climate-driven shifts in water column stratification directly influence nutrient replenishment and the timing of spring blooms. For instance, in the northwest Pacific Oyashio front region, spring bloom onsets oscillate between warm and cold anomalies, affecting zooplankton and commercial fisheries ([IOCCG Report](https://ioccg.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/ioccg-report-08.pdf); [Chiba et al., 2009](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1365-2486.2009.01890.x)).

## Synthesis & Data Governance
Synergizing spaceborne radiometers, ocean color sensors, and biogeochemical ocean models enables continuous surveillance of marine productivity and vulnerability. As marine heatwaves increase in intensity and frequency, integrated observations provide fisheries management and conservation authorities with early indicators of ecosystem regime shifts.
